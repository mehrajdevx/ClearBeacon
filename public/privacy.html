Privacy notice

Draft prepared 16 October 2026 · English only

This draft describes the current ClearBeacon prototype based on its code and configuration. Actual hosting, access, and legal responsibilities must be confirmed before launch. ClearBeacon is an independent educational project, not a government service or security guarantee. ClearBeacon is a provisional name pending legal clearance.

Who operates this service

Project operator: Fatima Abid, operating as an individual in Pakistan. The operator confirmed this is their full legal name and that no registered organization is currently operating the project.

Privacy contact: Not set up. A dedicated, monitored public contact method must be provided before launch. Do not submit a privacy request to this prototype expecting a response.

The operator's stated goal is global availability, but specific launch countries and legal service areas have not been decided. The current help directory lists resources for the United States, United Kingdom, Australia, Spain, Mexico, Pakistan, United Arab Emirates, Canada, India, France, and Bangladesh. Those reference links do not establish service availability or legal coverage in those or other countries.

Information processed

  • Link and message checks: the text you submit is sent to this service for local rule analysis. The application does not save the submitted URL or message, visit the URL, or send it to a threat-intelligence provider. Do not paste secrets, personal data, or private links.
  • High-risk scan signals: when a submitted link or message triggers a high-risk local rule or a known local-feed match, the database keeps a short generated evidence summary, scan type, severity, and timestamp for reviewer follow-up. The submitted text and URL are not included. Only the 25 most recent signals are retained; these signals are not independently verified.
  • Optional image helper: supported browsers process images on your device for QR/text extraction; the image is not uploaded. Availability and recognition quality vary.
  • Reports: the database stores the selected category, optional target, incident summary, optional evidence notes, contact channel preference, incident date, language, report status, a private reference, and related review records such as appeals, clarification messages, reviewer notes, and moderation history. The form does not request an email address or phone number.
  • Check feedback: if you send feedback, the database stores its explanation, check type, language, result label, and a SHA-256 digest of the checked input, not the original checked text. A digest can sometimes be matched against guesses and should not be treated as anonymous.
  • Browser storage: your selected language and up to ten recent result summaries are stored in this browser's local storage. Original checked URLs and messages are not stored in that history. Clear browser storage to remove it.
  • Technical data: the application suppresses its own HTTP request-path logs because paths can contain private report references. To rate-limit repeated requests, it keeps client IP addresses and request timestamps in process memory for the request window; stale entries are periodically pruned. The deployment environment may also produce network, security, or hosting logs; this prototype does not establish what an eventual hosting provider will collect or retain.

Why information is used and who can see it

Checks are used to return decision-support guidance. High-risk signals are surfaced to authorized reviewers, who must verify them independently; checks are user-triggered, not background monitoring. Reports and optional feedback are used for private review and service improvement. Authorized reviewers can access report details and moderation records as needed for that work. No report is automatically made public. Reviewer session tokens are kept in server memory and expire after 30 minutes. The application does not currently configure advertising, analytics, email, or an external URL-checking provider.

If you follow a help-directory link, you leave this service and the linked organization applies its own privacy practices. The current prototype does not otherwise disclose check inputs to those organizations.

Retention and deletion

Reports and check feedback are deleted from the live database after 365 days by default. The operator can configure a different period from 1 to 3,650 days. Reporters can also request deletion of a report and its linked review records using the private report reference. Keep that reference confidential.

High-risk scan signals are not linked to a report or private reference. The database retains up to the 25 most recent signals, replacing older entries when new ones arrive; no calendar-based expiry is currently configured.

Deleting a record from the live database does not remove it from backups already made. The prototype does not yet define a backup expiry schedule or propagate individual deletion requests to backups. Those arrangements must be documented and implemented before launch.

Security and service status

This supervised classroom demo currently runs on Vercel using a Python serverless function and SQLite files in temporary, per-instance storage. Data and reviewer sessions may be unavailable or lost when a function instance is replaced, and separate instances may not share state. There is no configured persistent database or backup. Vercel's function region and its processing, logging, and retention details have not been reviewed for a public service. The project has not completed production security, legal, privacy, or monitoring review. Use synthetic examples only; do not submit real reports, personal data, private links, or other sensitive information.

Your choices and requests

You can avoid submitting optional reports or feedback, clear local browser storage, and use a private report reference to export or delete your report. The privacy contact and process for other data-rights requests are not yet established. Applicable rights and response procedures depend on the operator and the laws that apply; those details require qualified review before launch.

Children and changes

The operator intends a general audience that may include minors. Age-related safeguards have not been determined. The operator must design and review suitable child-protection and privacy safeguards before launch. The final notice should show its effective date and explain how material changes will be communicated.
